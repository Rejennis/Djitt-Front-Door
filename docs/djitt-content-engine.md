# DJITT content engine

## Purpose

The DJITT public content model should not be built around tools, features or software categories alone. It should be built around business problems, context and the system solutions needed to resolve them.

The central sequence is:

BUSINESS PROBLEM → INSIGHT → DJITT FRAMEWORK → RESOURCE → SYSTEM → FRONT DOOR → GHL

This keeps the public website useful to the visitor while preserving a clean separation between discovery, education and qualification.

## Core editorial model

### 1. Business problem

Start from the real issue the visitor is facing.

Examples:

- We are not following up with prospects quickly enough.
- We are getting enquiries but not converting them into conversations.
- Our business depends too much on manual admin and memory.
- We have tools, but they do not work as one system.
- We know AI is relevant, but we need a practical way to use it.

### 2. Insight

Turn the problem into a clear insight that frames the pattern.

Examples:

- The Response Gap
- The Follow-Up Gap
- The Architecture Gap
- The Judgement Gap
- Digital Debt

### 3. DJITT framework

Translate the insight into a simple framework that gives the visitor language to understand the problem.

Examples:

- Speed-to-Trust
- Architecture Gap
- Digital Debt
- Fidelity Ceiling
- Resourcefulness Pivot
- Productive Delusion
- Contribution Call

### 4. Resource

Give the visitor a practical tool or guide that helps them understand the issue or assess their own situation.

Examples:

- Lead Follow-Up Readiness Checklist
- Client Acquisition Readiness Checklist
- Website Readiness Checklist
- Business Systems Assessment
- ICP Worksheet

### 5. System

Link the problem and insight to a relevant DJITT business system.

Examples:

- Client Acquisition System
- Workflow & Operations System
- AI Systems
- Business Systems
- Website Front Door

### 6. Front Door

Route the visitor toward the specific public front-door solution that matches their situation.

Examples:

- Improve Client Acquisition
- Improve Your Follow-Up
- Improve Your Operations
- Explore AI Systems
- Connect Your Business Systems
- Not Sure

### 7. GHL

Use the GHL / qualification path only after the visitor has been routed to the relevant business issue and has expressed intent.

This keeps GHL as the operational conversion layer, not the public website hero narrative.

## Example flow

Problem: We are not following up with prospects quickly enough.

Insight: The Response Gap

Framework: Speed-to-Trust

Resource: Lead Follow-Up Readiness Checklist

System: Client Acquisition System

Front Door: Improve Client Acquisition

Action: Practice Score / consultation qualification in GHL

## Editorial principles

- Lead with the business problem, not the technology.
- Keep the language specific, practical and credible.
- Do not use generic agency hype or unsupported numerical claims.
- Do not position DJITT as providing financial advice.
- Do not treat software tools as the story; treat them as components inside a system.
- Maintain a consistent vocabulary across website copy, system pages, insights and resources.

## Content engine outcome

The objective is to make the website feel like a coherent system that moves visitors through a clear sequence:

- identify the business problem
- understand the pattern
- learn the framework
- access the relevant resource
- recognise the relevant system
- choose the matching front-door path
- continue into GHL when intent is clear

This creates a stronger public knowledge architecture and keeps the site aligned with the second-generation DJITT positioning.
