# Our Journey: From DJITT Platform to DJITT Front Door

This document captures how the DJITT direction evolved from the earlier platform-first website and the original business model into the current Front Door architecture.

## 1) Where we started

The original DJITT website positioned the business around a clear, platform-led story:

- AI-powered business systems
- automated client acquisition
- CRM and sales pipeline automation
- follow-up systems
- content and lead generation
- a central DJITT platform

This was a strong and useful foundation for the internal strategy. It communicated that DJITT was not simply a marketing agency; it was a systems business focused on intelligent operational growth.

The original structure naturally led with:

- platform capability
- capture / nurture / close
- services and features
- implementation workflow
- financial-adviser positioning

This matched an earlier stage of the business model, where DJITT was being explained as a coherent operating system for financial advisers.

## 2) Why the original model started to feel too narrow

As the business model matured, the logic shifted. The problem was not that the platform was wrong. It was that the public-facing architecture no longer matched how DJITT was evolving.

The older site made DJITT sound like:

- a SaaS product
- a software suite
- a service catalogue
- a platform you buy into

That is useful for an internal product story, but it is less effective for a public site that must serve multiple audiences.

At the same time, we had already identified a deeper strategic truth:

- DJITT should not be explained only as a product
- DJITT should be explained as an entry system that routes people to the right solution
- different customers need different starting points
- the model is broader than one vertical or one single buyer type

## 3) The strategic shift

The key realisation was this:

DJITT should not lead with software. It should lead with the business problem.

Instead of saying:

> "Here is our platform and here are the services."

we moved toward:

> "What do you need to improve? We will route you to the right DJITT path."

This marks the shift from product-led messaging to problem-led messaging.

## 4) The Front Door principle

The Front Door became the critical concept.

The Front Door is not just a page or a form. It is a routing layer.

It answers the question:

> "What is the business trying to solve right now?"

From there, the visitor can be directed into the appropriate route:

- website / digital visibility
- lead generation and lead follow-up
- growth system design
- financial adviser route
- broader vertical services
- future vertical systems

This is why the Front Door is more powerful than simply renaming an old page.

It becomes the universal entry point for DJITT.

## 5) Why website-only leads needed their own path

The direct website / SME service offer was always a valid route, but it needed to be separated from the deeper DJITT platform story.

A visitor looking for a website is usually not asking:

- what is your full AI operating system?
- what is your platform architecture?
- what is your capture / nurture / close framework?

They are asking:

- do you build websites?
- can you make my business look professional online?
- can you get me more enquiries?
- can you help me look more credible?

This created a second path:

- Website only path
- Front Door path for broader business problems
- financial adviser path for the current strong ICP

This was a major step away from one-size-fits-all messaging.

## 6) Why the architecture became clearer

Once we separated the routes, the site became easier to reason about:

- Home = entry
- Front Door = routing
- Websites = direct service offer
- Resources = education and authority
- Insights = strategic thinking and framework content
- Case Studies = proof and credibility
- advisor flow remains separate and intact

This made the public website feel like a real digital front office rather than a generic marketing page or an incomplete product launch page.

## 7) The move toward authority and discoverability

The next shift was also important. The site began to align with a more durable public-positioning model:

- search and AI discoverability
- content clusters and authority pages
- different entry paths for different intent types
- a public layer that can lead into GHL and CRM workflows
- a cleaner separation between marketing site and operating system

That is why the resource pages and insights pages matter. They are not decorative. They create the public layer of trust and expertise around the DJITT story.

## 8) Why this is more correct than the original platform-first structure

The current model is stronger because it matches the reality of the business:

- DJITT is bigger than one product
- different customers need different paths
- public positioning needs to be more accessible than internal architecture
- the website should route people intelligently rather than overwhelm them
- the financial adviser route remains important without blocking all other routes

The Front Door is therefore not a cosmetic change. It is the logical evolution of the DJITT brand from a product-led story into a routing-led architecture.

## 9) The final architecture we moved to

The current direction can be represented as:

- Public website on Netlify
- Website and Front Door routes on the public site
- GHL as the CRM and operational system
- advisor acquisition pipeline left intact
- new Front Door and Website Leads pipelines kept separate
- content and authority pages supporting SEO / AEO / GEO

This gives DJITT a cleaner and more scalable operating model.

## 10) Summary

The journey was:

1. Start with a platform-first DJITT story.
2. Recognise the platform was valid but too product-led for the public site.
3. Separate the problem from the product.
4. Introduce the Front Door as the universal routing layer.
5. Create direct website and service entry points.
6. Build authority and content layers around those routes.
7. Keep the advisor acquisition system separate and protected.
8. Deliver a cleaner architecture that fits how DJITT is actually growing.

That is the path from where we started to where we are now.
